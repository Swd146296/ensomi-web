What Ensomi is aiming for

Ensomi wants listening to music, playing it and creating for it to become one activity. Three scenarios carry that aim.

Ambient play

Music is already playing around you. Ensomi recognises it, finds the current position and plays a chart, generated in real time, that follows the music without interrupting it.

Mapping

A mapper selects one section and asks for candidate choreography while the rest of the chart stays in place. Candidates are compared, kept, retried or undone. Choice and expression stay with the mapper.

Training

A player picks music they like and a movement to practise, and the chart is shaped around it.

That needs charts that are playable at the requested difficulty, follow the music's rhythm, keep one character through the whole song, and respond to requests. R2 is the first step: a whole song, generated while you wait. It does not yet play along in real time or regenerate one section of an existing chart.

What R2 does today

R2 is the first Ensomi release that works from audio alone. A 2 to 3.3 minute song takes 8 to 12 seconds on the machine it was checked on (an Apple M5, CPU, one thread).

  1. Timing systemFinds the beats with BeatThis, then refits each tempo segment to the song's own note onsets. No trained weights of its own.
  2. Rows model2.1M parameters. Beat by beat, it chooses where notes start from what it hears.
  3. Corpus priorPicks the chart's character, such as long-note level and chord density, from the 64 human charts whose head rows look most alike.
  4. R22.4M parameters. Arranges lanes, taps and long notes on every head row, then writes the .osz. It does not hear the audio.

Preview charts

Four songs, generated at seed 0 with default settings. They illustrate behaviour; they are not a quality benchmark. No audio is included. Each image is the middle page of the chart's render by Beatmap Lens.

Song titles and artists are those of the songs. The generated .osu files are in the gallery on Hugging Face.

What works and what does not yet

Every chart passes legality and replay checks and has whole-millisecond times. Quality was judged by one experienced player in blind pairs, one generation seed per side, so a single pair is weak evidence.

RequestStatusEvidence
Star rating for the whole songWorksBoth blind pairs called the difficulty the right way.
Jumpstream and chordstream sectionsWorksWork in blind pairs.
Stream and jack sectionsMeasured onlyThey reach their target in measurement; not screened on their own.
Section difficulty in starsNot screenedSections asked to be easier land short of their target.
Chordjack sectionsNot offeredOut of band in 18 of 28 runs on generated rows.
Long-note amount (--ln tap|half|natural)Does not work yet"Half" removes about a third, and a blind pair could not tell.

The human chart still wins. In all three reference pairs, the human chart of the same song was chosen over R2's. On this evidence R2 has not reached human charts.

Checked

  • The new rows model beat the earlier head generator 10 to 3, one undecided.
  • On a holdout of 519 human charts, 85% have 90% of their heads within 20 ms of the timing system's grid.
  • The four example charts regenerate note for note from the published files, with BeatThis freshly downloaded.

Known limits

  • Long notes are the weakest part.
  • Easier sections land short of their target.
  • Long-song stability rests on a decode-time rest rule, not on training.
  • Every verdict is one listener's.

The evidence for each claim is in the report.

Run it yourself

There is no in-browser demo yet. The next steps are to plug the system into the Ensomi macOS app, and to design one model runtime shared by that app and a future web app. Until then R2 runs from the command line. It was checked on macOS arm64 (Apple M5, Python 3.10, torch 2.11, CPU); Linux and CUDA were not run.

git clone https://github.com/ensomi-labs/ensomi-model.git
cd ensomi-model
git checkout --detach r2-1.0
uv sync --python 3.10 --extra mps
hf download sed-i/ensomi-r2 --local-dir artifacts/hf-r2

uv run --python 3.10 --extra mps python -m ensomi_model.system.make \
  --model-dir artifacts/hf-r2 --audio /path/to/song.mp3 --star 3.5 \
  --title "Song title" --artist "Artist" --seed 0 --out artifacts/my-chart.osz

The first run also downloads BeatThis's checkpoint (77 MB) from its authors. Decoding audio other than WAV needs ffmpeg. --help lists every request. Full instructions are on the Hugging Face card.
